The opportunities, in order, are **drafting the update**, then **assisting its factual review** by linking each claim to an excerpt and flagging gaps. Both belong to the same small experiment. There is no evidence here for automating the stakeholder conversations, Priya’s learning, or migration decisions.

**First experiment: pasted excerpts → grounded draft → human review.**

Start with an authorized historical copy of one Friday’s five excerpts, numbered 1–5. Ask the agent to produce a short stakeholder update covering progress, blockers and stated next steps, plus a claim-to-source checklist and unresolved questions. Require it to use only supplied facts, identify conflicting reports, and leave missing details explicit. It must not invent dates, commitments or owners, or decide which priority wins.

You own the update and its final approval. An engineer checks technical factual details; you check meaning, tone, completeness and any commitments before sending. The tools are simply an agent and pasted text. No connectors, deployment or recurring automation are needed.

Check that every factual claim is supported, important blockers are retained, and uncertainty stays visible. In a copy of the input, test a conflicting status and a missing owner or date: the draft should flag them rather than resolve or fill them in. Human review determines whether it helped; an agent’s self-assessment is not proof.

If the historical trial produces a useful draft with manageable corrections, try the same manual process on the next two Fridays. Stop or adjust it if unsupported claims recur, important issues disappear, or preparation plus review and correction takes as long as—or longer than—the manual workflow. Do not send an unchecked draft.

Your measured baseline is **40 active minutes per Friday on average**, ranging from 35 to 45 minutes across four weeks, for gathering and first drafting only. Review and maintenance time are unknown, so no net saving is established. During the trial, record source gathering/pasting, drafting, review, corrections and prompt upkeep separately. Compare gathering-plus-drafting with the existing baseline; measure a manual full cycle as well before claiming a total time saving. Include the engineer’s review effort, factual errors and rework, and ask stakeholders whether the update remains clear and useful.

The intended benefit is more room to coach people. If capacity returns, deliberately use some for coaching and observe whether that space actually appears; do not assume faster drafting automatically creates it or turn it into another delivery commitment.
